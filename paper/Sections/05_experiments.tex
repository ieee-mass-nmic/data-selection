\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation tests whether PCU-Select preserves per-PEFT LESS's average downstream quality, reduces the marginal cost of adding a PEFT, and transfers to new configurations according to their structural support in the training registry. We then identify which components sustain the reusable scorer.

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate Llama-2-7B~\cite{touvron2023llama2} on GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU, and TyDiQA~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k,chen2021humaneval,hendrycks2021mmlu,clark2020tydiqa}, using exact match, Pass@1, accuracy, and F1, respectively. We report percentages and their equal-weight macro-average as a registry-level summary. Per-task results appear in Supplementary Table~S1. Every selector chooses 10\% from a decontaminated pool of 300K mixed instruction examples. Each task sketch contains 32 fixed-seed examples from the task's training or development split and remains disjoint from evaluation. HumanEval uses MBPP and CodeAlpaca because it lacks a training split; content hashes and AST-normalized signatures remove all sketch examples from the candidate pool. The pool combines seven public instruction corpora after exact, normalized-string, 13-gram, and MinHash decontamination; each selected subset contains 30K examples.

The main comparison covers five configurations seen during scorer training: attention and MLP LoRA, (IA)$^3$, and adapters. The transfer study holds out configurations that vary rank, placement, learning rate, or family, including Prefix Tuning, P-Tuning, and LN Tuning~\cite{wang2022lntuning}. We instantiate LN Tuning by updating the scale parameters of both per-block RMSNorm modules and excluding the final model-level RMSNorm~\cite{touvron2023llama2}. Supplementary Table~S3 lists all 17 configurations.

The four baselines differ in target specificity. \textbf{Random} uses neither task nor PEFT information. \textbf{RDS+}, our representation-similarity baseline, ranks frozen semantic embeddings by similarity to the task-sketch mean but ignores the PEFT configuration. \textbf{Influence}, our shared-datastore gradient baseline, ranks projected base-model gradients from one PEFT-agnostic datastore. \textbf{LESS}, our per-PEFT implementation of gradient-influence selection~\cite{xia2024less}, warms up each target adapter, builds an Adam-preconditioned gradient datastore, and ranks examples by sketch influence. PCU-Select uses its conditional scorer, uncertainty penalty, and adaptive semantic-cluster quotas.

We hold the backbone, PEFT recipe, training steps, and evaluation protocol constant, so only the selected subset changes. Target adaptation trains only PEFT parameters for 1000 AdamW steps with a cosine schedule, global batch 128, and maximum sequence length 1024. All costs use eight NVIDIA H20-96GB GPUs and the wall-time-times-GPU-count convention. The main table varies target-training seeds 0, 1, and 2 while fixing the selector, scorer, sketch, and pool at seed 0. Matched seeds give every selector the same training randomness. We obtain each descriptive 95\% interval by resampling the evaluated PEFT--task cells under this fixed selection pipeline.

\subsection{PCU-Select Near-Ties Per-PEFT LESS}
\label{sec:quality}

\input{tables/table_main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} shows that PCU-Select improves on the PEFT-agnostic baselines and remains close to target-specific LESS; Supplementary Table~S1 gives task-wise aggregates and win counts for the 20 PEFT$\times$task cells. It gains 0.74 points over RDS+ (17/20 wins; interval $[+0.38,+1.06]$) and 0.50 over Influence (18/20; $[+0.21,+0.75]$). Against LESS, its mean difference is $+0.04$ points ($[-0.26,+0.33]$; 10/20 wins).

Performance varies across tasks and PEFTs, so an average near-tie is the appropriately scoped registry-level conclusion. The task-grouped interval $[-0.15,+0.22]$ also crosses zero.

\subsection{Amortization Cuts Four-Task PEFT Onboarding Cost by 42$\times$}
\label{sec:reuse}

\input{tables/table_quality_cost_headline}

Table~\ref{tab:quality-cost-headline} separates \emph{fixed/shared} cost, paid once, from \emph{marginal} cost, paid for each new PEFT. All selector costs exclude the shared 12.8 GPU-hour downstream run. PCU-Select and per-PEFT LESS have near-tied average quality but distribute selection cost differently. LESS has no fixed cost and spends 63.2 GPU-hours per four-task configuration. PCU-Select pays 144.0 GPU-hours once, then 1.51 GPU-hours per new configuration: a $42\times$ lower marginal cost.

The gap comes from configuration reuse. Adding a task to an existing PEFT costs one online-selection pass: 0.38 GPU-hours for PCU-Select and 0.30 for LESS. The PCU-Select value rounds the exact average per-task cost of 0.3775. Adding a PEFT forces LESS to rebuild its datastore, whereas PCU-Select reuses the scorer (Supplementary Table~S5). The cost benefit therefore comes from cross-PEFT reuse.

The fixed-subset shortcut trades away quality. \textbf{Reuse-one-LESS} applies one LESS subset to all five targets; it costs 63.2 GPU-hours but averages 34.24, below RDS+ at 34.44. Reusing a subset selected for one trainable subspace removes the target condition. PCU-Select produces a distinct subset for each target and retains LESS-level average quality.

The measured costs break even at 2.33 four-task configurations. Serving the five-PEFT registry costs 151.6 GPU-hours with PCU-Select and 316.0 with LESS, a $2.09\times$ reduction.
For targets far from the training registry, optional calibration uses 500 \emph{reduced calibration labels} from one warm anchor and one update step. This procedure costs 1.05 GPU-hours per $(p,t)$, one quarter of the $\approx4.2$ GPU-hours for the full routine. It shifts the worst-case break-even point to 2.50 configurations. For supported four-task configurations that require no calibration, PCU-Select's marginal selection cost is $42\times$ lower than per-PEFT LESS.

\subsection{Reduced Calibration Narrows Gaps Beyond the Training Registry}
\label{sec:transfer}

Before evaluation, we group held-out targets by structural support: L0 resembles a seen configuration, L1 makes a larger shift within a seen family, and L2 introduces an unseen family.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.76\columnwidth]{Figures/fig_ood_calibration.pdf}
\caption{Mean LESS-minus-PCU-Select gap across each tier's targets and three tasks (lower is better). Cal-200/500 denote calibration-label counts.}
\label{fig:ood-calibration}
\end{figure}

Cal-200/500 freeze the shared scorer and fit only a zero-initialized linear residual head using 200/500 target-specific short-update labels. Because each label uses one warm anchor and one update, the two budgets cost only 0.42 and 1.05 GPU-hours per PEFT--task pair.

Against LESS, the zero-shot gap grows from 0.34 points at L0 to 2.08 at L1, 4.08 for LN Tuning, and 6.97 for Prefix/P-Tuning (Figure~\ref{fig:ood-calibration}). Cal-200 already cuts the L1 and LN-Tuning gaps to 0.91 and 0.65; Cal-500 further lowers them to 0.30 and 0.23, removing 86\% and 94\% of the original gaps. The additional 300 labels yield only 0.61 and 0.42 points of recovery, indicating that a lightweight residual correction recovers most of the shared scorer's transferable ranking structure. The Prefix/P-Tuning gap falls from 6.97 in the zero-shot setting to 2.17 with Cal-200 and 1.99 with Cal-500; its 0.18-point Cal-200-to-Cal-500 change is descriptive.

\subsection{Ablations Identify the Useful Components}
\label{sec:ablation}

Removing the PEFT code causes the largest downstream drop, 1.37 points; removing the task sketch costs 0.82 points. A family-only code loses 0.68 points, and uniform rather than adaptive cluster quotas loses 0.51, showing that configuration structure and value-aware coverage both matter. Using only gradient-proxy or short-update labels loses 0.85 or 0.36 points, respectively. Replacing adaptive quotas with global top-$k$ loses 1.08 points, while removing the activation signature or uncertainty penalty costs 0.48 or 0.28 points. Supplementary Table~S9 reports the full ablation on GSM8K and HumanEval with two representative PEFTs.

\subsection{Limitations}

Evaluation remains limited to one backbone family, four tasks, and a finite registry; broader backbones, domains, and update mechanisms require validation. Short-update labels approximate full-training contribution, and structural tiers are registry-relative. Finally, amortization is most relevant when a service repeatedly onboards configurations rather than serving a single target.
